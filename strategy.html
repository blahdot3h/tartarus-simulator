The short version

  1. Consume every day you can.Waiting to build a bigger chain cost Rainbows: per run with a small threshold and with a large one, against when you always consume (no tools).The one exception: don't break a prey mark. When a Lion marks a prey of its own rarity, both rise a tier every morning for free until the mark runs out. Eating the marked prey ends the mark, and so does letting the marked Lion consume, since it usually turns into another beast. Either way you trade several free tiers for one. A consume almost never loses value otherwise: two inmates of one rarity become one a tier higher, which is worth more than the pair. In the contract challenge, also keep the inmates the next contract needs.

A day on the block

Every day runs in the same order. Here is what the strategy does at each step.

  1. Patrol

    Pick one of the three destinations. A locked door usually wins: it adds a new inmate, who may consume today from the warden's cell. Go to a known inmate instead when it has a strong consume waiting. A door with an event badge is a bonus. Prefer the farther destination when they're close: it counts toward Prison Walk, and with the Long-Chained Shackles a walk of 4 or more cells can raise an inmate a tier. Don't open a rat nest before it hatches.

  2. Event

    Accept Ravenous Appetite, High-Risk Convict, Crow Whispers, Taste of Freedom and the Usurpations, and Behind Closed Doors unless its inmate is already a Supreme. The "Treasure" Hunt has only Confirm: afterwards, the locked doors it leaves dark hold Rare or better. Chain reactions are the close call above. For an Execution, count the inmates it would remove against the tiers the executioner gains, and accept only if you come out ahead.

  3. Consume

    If the warden's inmate has a target, consume. Pick the first meal whose chain is worth the most: the Trainer shows each option's chain odds. After that bite, your game asks for each next meal, and its Cancel stops the chain. Going on is almost always right, so stop only when the Trainer says so. When the eater has more than one meal, pick the one whose next tier still has food for the beast it may re-roll into.

  4. Replace

    If the warden's inmate is low-tier and has nothing worth eating, replace it. A day has a Replace or a consume, not both, so a good consume comes first.

  5. End the day

    Once nothing in the warden's cell can gain, end the day. The next morning, prey marks grow, nests hatch, and new destinations are drawn.

Chains: how many beasts you need

After every bite the eater rises a tier and re-rolls into a random beast. The chain continues if that new beast has something to eat at its new tier. Each beast eats exactly one other: a Mammoth needs a Lion, a Lion needs a Wolf, a Wolf needs a Rat, and a Rat needs a Mammoth. So each beast waiting at a tier covers one of the four possible re-rolls.

The contract challenge

Twelve days, three contracts and 6 vouchers. You pass by delivering two contracts, one of them the heavy contract, which needs a Supreme or a vaulted Rainbow. Every inmate you send scores 2 to the power of its tier and adds about twice that in convoy pressure. So a light escort (1 voucher, pressure up to 36) carries at most 18 points, and a heavy escort (3 vouchers, up to 72) at most 36. A run tops out near 72 points, plus any bounty.

Convoy rules

  1. Never spend the heavy contract’s last voucher.The heavy contract needs at least one voucher, and the run can’t pass without it. Sending the most points on every contract can spend all six before day 12. That habit passed only of runs.
  2. Send the first two contracts by light escort.That keeps 3 vouchers for the heavy contract, enough for a heavy escort: the only way to ship a Rainbow (pressure 65), and the way to 36 points. Allowing heavy escorts on the first two passed , against .
  3. Send only what the contract needs.On the first two contracts, load the cheapest inmates that meet it, plus any spare Normals, and keep everything else for later.
  4. Get ready over the last before each delivery window.Check what the next contract needs and play for it: open doors when a Work gang or Mixed convoy needs more inmates, and hold on to Rares and Legendaries for the day-8 contract. Ignoring the next contract passed , against .
  5. Fill the heavy convoy.The heavy contract ends the run, so send the most points a heavy escort can carry: two Supremes and a Rare, or a Rainbow with a Magic and a Normal.

How these numbers were made. Every run used the simulator's own rules engine. A rules-based strategy played them, making each choice from what a player can see. There's no AI involved: it's a set of scoring rules whose weights were tuned for these rules by trying each value and keeping whichever vaulted more Rainbows. This data set took runs and minutes. The rules follow the season's video reveal and what players have since seen live. The rarity odds come from players' shared boards: an inmate behind a door is 40% Normal, 38% Magic, 12% Rare, 8% Legendary and 2% Supreme, and a Replace brings in 52%, 32%, 9%, 6% and 1.5%. Other odds and limits are practice values and may differ from the live game: 5% of sealed cells hide an event, every kind equally likely, which shows only once the warden walks past, and each day has one consume or one Replace, not both, over a 20-day drill.

A day on the block

Every day runs in the same order. Here is what the strategy does at each step.

  1. Patrol

    Pick one of the three destinations. A locked door usually wins: it adds a new inmate, who may consume today from the warden's cell. Go to a known inmate instead when it has a strong consume waiting. A door with an event badge is a bonus. Prefer the farther destination when they're close: it counts toward Prison Walk, and with the Long-Chained Shackles a walk of 4 or more cells can raise an inmate a tier. Don't open a rat nest before it hatches.

  2. Event

    Accept Ravenous Appetite, High-Risk Convict, Crow Whispers, Taste of Freedom, Extra Portion and the Usurpations, and Behind Closed Doors unless its inmate is already a Supreme. The "Treasure" Hunt has only Confirm: afterwards, the locked doors it leaves dark hold Rare or better. Chain reactions are the close call above. For an Execution, count the inmates it would remove against the tiers the executioner gains, and accept only if you come out ahead.

  3. Consume

    If the warden's inmate has a target, consume. Pick the first meal whose chain is worth the most: the Trainer shows each option's chain odds. After that bite, your game asks for each next meal, and its Cancel stops the chain. Going on is almost always right, so stop only when the Trainer says so. When the eater has more than one meal, pick the one whose next tier still has food for the beast it may re-roll into.

  4. Replace

    If the warden's inmate is low-tier and has nothing worth eating, replace it. A day has a Replace or a consume, not both, so a good consume comes first.

  5. End the day

    Once nothing in the warden's cell can gain, end the day. The next morning, prey marks grow, nests hatch, and new destinations are drawn.

Chains: how many beasts you need

After every bite the eater rises a tier and re-rolls into a random beast. The chain continues if that new beast has something to eat at its new tier. A Mammoth eats Lions and Wolves, a Lion Wolves and Rats, a Wolf Rats, and a Rat Mammoths. So a Wolf or a Rat waiting at a tier feeds two of the four possible re-rolls, a Lion or a Mammoth one, and a Mammoth, a Rat and a Lion or a Wolf together feed all four.

How these numbers were made. Every run used the simulator's own rules engine. A rules-based strategy played them, making each choice from what a player can see. There's no AI involved: it's a set of scoring rules whose weights were tuned for these rules by trying each value and keeping whichever vaulted more Rainbows. This data set took runs and minutes. The rules follow the season's video reveal and what players have since seen live. The rarity odds come from players' shared boards: an inmate behind a door is 40% Normal, 38% Magic, 12% Rare, 8% Legendary and 2% Supreme, and a Replace brings in 42%, 34%, 11.5%, 9.5% and 2.5%, always of another beast. From the same boards: a run starts with 4 inmates out (3 in about 1 run in 20), 72% of runs start with a special event, each event the warden sets off is followed by a new one 88% of the time, and the warden's office comes up among the doors about 1 morning in 4 when it's in reach. Other odds and limits are practice values and may differ from the live game: each day has one consume or one Replace, not both, over a 20-day drill. The full tables are in the strategy lab's notes.
